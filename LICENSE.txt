5. WHAT BEETHOVEN DOES WITH YOUR CREDENTIALS AND YOUR WORK

This section describes the software as shipped, so that you can hold Licensor to it.

(a) Claude. Beethoven never reads, stores, copies, prints or relays a Claude credential. You sign in
to Claude Code yourself, through Anthropic's own flow, once, in a terminal on a compute node of your
own cluster account. The Extension starts that terminal for you through your portal's shell, in the
same command that starts your worker, opens Anthropic's sign-in page in a new tab, shows you one
box, and closes that tab once Claude Code reports you are signed in, unless the tab has loaded
another page since you pasted the code; you sign in on Anthropic's page and paste the one-time code
it shows into that box. The Extension types the code once into that terminal and keeps no copy of
it: it is not stored, not written to the audit log and not sent to Licensor or anyone else, and the
Extension never reads Anthropic's page or your clipboard to obtain it. Claude Code makes its
credential from the code and keeps it in its own folder in your cluster account; the Framework only
starts Claude Code where that credential already is, and Beethoven reads Claude Code's own report of
whether it is signed in, with the account and plan that report names, nothing more. You use one
Claude account, your own. You answer Claude Code's one-time question about Remote Control yourself,
with the box you tick in the Extension's panel before your first worker starts; the Framework
records that answer in one file in your cluster account when you press Connect, and your worker
gives it to Claude Code when Claude Code asks. Trust for the worker's own folder is recorded in
Claude Code's settings by the Framework when a worker starts. Beethoven gives Claude Code no answer
you have not given.

(b) Your portal session. The Extension uses the sign-in your browser already holds for your
institution's Open OnDemand portal, on that one website only, from its own background and without a
portal tab where your browser allows it. It never reads that sign-in, never reads, stores or types a
portal password, never signs in for you and never touches a multi-factor step. When the portal asks
for a sign-in, the Extension opens the portal in a tab for you to sign in there (in your browser's
own sign-in box, or on the portal's own sign-in page), never sees what you type there, and closes
that tab once you are signed in, while it is still on the portal. Where your portal does not share
its sign-in with the Extension, it keeps that tab behind your other tabs until the work in hand is
done and then closes it, and for a later action opens the portal in a background tab and closes it
again. The one thing it reads about you from the portal is your account name, from the portal's own
dashboard page. If your portal's shell asks for your cluster password, as it may for an account that
has no key on the cluster, the Extension types nothing there and offers a one-time step instead: it
opens the portal's own terminal page in a tab, and you type your password there yourself; the
Extension never sees it. Once you say you are in, or the page's title shows that your shell is up,
the Extension types one line into that terminal (or leaves it on your clipboard for you to paste),
which runs the Framework's login-setup step in your own account. That step makes a key in your
cluster account, in its ~/.ssh folder, that opens your account only from the computer the terminal
came from, which at your institution is its Open OnDemand server (it is a file in your own account,
protected as your other files are, and hidden from Claude Code inside your worker; anyone who
obtained it and could connect from that server could use it), and has ssh offer it when the portal's
shell connects you, so that the shell need not ask for your password; it never reads, changes or
prints a key you already have. The key stays in your cluster account: the Extension never sees it,
keeps nothing about it in your browser and sends nothing about it to Licensor. The command
"hpcworker login-setup --remove", run in a terminal on the cluster, removes it. This step has been
tried only against the Licensor's mock portal, not yet on a real cluster by an account without a
key.

(c) Your account name, as the portal's dashboard shows it, and the answer of the last approval check
with its time are stored in the Extension's own storage in your browser. Nothing about your approval
is copied to your cluster account; the Framework has no key, password or account list of Licensor's.

(d) The Framework makes a random token for each job so that only your own browser session can reach
your worker. It is kept in a file only you can read, is never shown, and never appears on a command
line or in a job log.

(e) Where your work goes. Files the worker reads and every command's output are sent to Anthropic.
While you are connected through claude.ai the transcript is also stored on Anthropic's servers. A
personal plan is retained five years if model training is on and 30 days otherwise. No BAA covers a
personal plan. FERPA records, IRB data and export-controlled technical data must not be in the
working directory. Remote Control usage draws from your plan's shared limit. The Extension shows
these sentences in short form, with the full text one click away, before your first worker starts,
and a one-line reminder beside Connect each time you start one, and does not start a worker in a
folder you have marked restricted.

(f) The Extension runs no code on claude.ai or any other Anthropic page and reads nothing from them.
Its only page script runs on the one portal website you connect: where your portal does not share
its sign-in with the Extension, to carry the Extension's requests there, and, during the one-time
step in (b), on the portal's terminal page, where it watches only the page's title and types only
the one line described there. It opens an Anthropic page only in a new tab, as a bookmark would: the
sign-in page during the sign-in described in (a), your worker's session page when a worker you
started is ready (unless you turn that off) and when you choose "Open my session" or "New session",
the connectors settings page if you choose the optional Google Drive step, and the plans page if you
follow "See plans" on the welcome page; it closes the sign-in tab it opened once you are signed in,
and it may bring forward a claude.ai tab it opened itself. Of an Anthropic tab it opened, it learns
only that the tab has begun loading a page, which the browser tells it without the page's address or
content.

(g) Of (a) to (f), only your account name in (c) is sent to Licensor, in the approval check that
section 10 describes; nothing else is. Section 10 describes the only things that may be.
