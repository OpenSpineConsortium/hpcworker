1. WHAT ASHLEY BRIDGE IS

"Ashley Bridge" means the browser extension of that name (the "Extension"), the scripts and programs
it asks you to install in your own computing account on your institution's cluster, or that your
institution's lab keeps in one shared copy which the Extension points your account at (either way,
the "Framework"), the recipe for the container image the Framework runs, its documentation, and
updates Licensor provides. The Extension starts, watches and stops a job in your own cluster account through
your institution's Open OnDemand portal; the job runs Claude Code, Anthropic's program, which you
have signed in to yourself, and makes that session visible to you at claude.ai and in the Claude app

uses for you, and there is nothing to type or paste. "Named Dependencies" are the outside parties and
conditions listed in section 7.

5. WHAT ASHLEY BRIDGE DOES WITH YOUR CREDENTIALS AND YOUR WORK

This section describes the software as shipped, so that you can hold Licensor to it.

(a) Claude. Ashley Bridge never reads, stores, copies, prints or relays a Claude credential. You sign
in to Claude Code yourself, through Anthropic's own flow, once, in a terminal on a compute node of
your own cluster account. The Extension starts that terminal for you through your portal's shell,
opens Anthropic's sign-in page in a new tab, and shows you one box; you sign in on Anthropic's page
and paste the one-time code it shows into that box. The Extension types the code once into that
terminal and keeps no copy of it: it is not stored, not written to the audit log and not sent to
Licensor or anyone else, and the Extension never reads Anthropic's page or your clipboard to obtain
it. Claude Code makes its credential from the code and keeps it in its own folder in your cluster
account; the Framework only starts Claude Code where that credential already is, and Ashley Bridge
reads Claude Code's yes-or-no answer about whether it is signed in, nothing more. You use one Claude
account, your own. You answer Claude Code's one-time question about Remote Control yourself, in the
Extension's own panel, before your first worker starts; the Extension records that answer in one file
in your cluster account, and your worker gives it to Claude Code when Claude Code asks. Trust for the
worker's own folder is recorded in Claude Code's settings by the Framework when a worker starts.
Ashley Bridge gives Claude Code no answer you have not given.

(b) Your portal session. The Extension uses the sign-in your browser already holds for your
institution's Open OnDemand portal, on that one website only, in the same way the portal's own pages
do. It never reads the sign-in cookie, never stores a portal password, never signs in for you and
never touches a multi-factor step. When the portal asks for a sign-in, the Extension asks you to sign
in in a tab. The one thing it reads about you from the portal is your account name, from the
portal's own dashboard page.

(c) Your account name, as the portal's dashboard shows it, and the answer of the last approval check
with its time are stored in the Extension's own storage in your browser. Nothing about your approval
is copied to your cluster account; the Framework has no key, password or account list of Licensor's.

(d) The Framework makes a random token for each job so that only your own browser session can reach
your worker. It is kept in a file only you can read, is never shown, and never appears on a command
line or in a job log.

(e) Where your work goes. Files the worker reads and every command's output are sent to Anthropic.
While you are connected through claude.ai the transcript is also stored on Anthropic's servers. A
personal plan is retained five years if model training is on and 30 days otherwise. No BAA covers a
personal plan. FERPA records, IRB data and export-controlled technical data must not be in the
working directory. Remote Control usage draws from your plan's shared limit. The Extension shows
these sentences at first run and before each job start, and does not start a worker in a folder you
have marked restricted.

(f) The Extension runs no code on claude.ai or any other Anthropic page and reads nothing from them.
Its only page script runs on the one portal website you connect. It opens an Anthropic page only in a
new tab, as a bookmark would: the sign-in page during the sign-in described in (a), your worker's
session page when the worker connects, and the connectors settings page if you choose the optional
Google Drive step.

(g) Of (a) to (f), only your account name in (c) is sent to Licensor, in the approval check that
section 10 describes; nothing else is. Section 10 describes the only things that may be.
